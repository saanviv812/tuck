three steps. that's the whole thing.

  1. CtrlShiftSpace

    press the shortcut

    from a browser, a doc, a game, anything. the popup opens on whichever screen you're looking at, already ready to type.

  2. what if…

    type the thought

    no title, no folder, no formatting. as long or short as you like. need a new line? Shift + Enter.

  3. Enter ↵

    press enter

    saved to your computer in about 50 ms. your buddy does a little hop, and the popup gets out of the way.

pick a buddy. the whole app follows.

nine of them. they watch your cursor, blink, breathe, look at what you type, hop when you save, and nap if you leave them alone. click one to see the page change colour, just like the app does.

small on purpose. still has everything.

a desk buddy
your buddy can live on top of your taskbar and wander a bit. double-click it to write. right-click it to make it nap, dance or stay put.
a library for later
every thought, newest first, grouped by day. search everything, tuck things into pockets (folders, but cosier), tag, edit, archive.
little suggestions
"this looks like an idea. tag it #onboarding?" yes or no thanks. simple word rules on your computer, no AI, and it never changes what you wrote.
drafts that stick around
closed the popup by accident? whatever you typed is still there next time.
updates itself
new versions download quietly. you click "restart to update" whenever you feel like it.
keyboard all the way

privacy.txt

your thoughts never leave your computer.

no account. no cloud. no tracking. no ads. everything lives in one plain file you can open in Notepad:

%APPDATA%\app.tuck.desktop\thoughts.json

it keeps a backup copy, and it never half-writes the file, so a crash or a power cut can't eat your thoughts. the only time tuck goes online is to check for an update.

questions

windows says "Windows protected your PC". is it safe?

windows shows that for apps it hasn't seen much of yet, and tuck is brand new. click More info, then Run anyway. tuck never sends your thoughts anywhere, and all of its code is open on GitHub for anyone to check.

how do the suggestions work without AI?

simple rules. "what if…" usually means an idea, "remember to…" a to-do, a web address a link. tags come from your #hashtags and the most distinctive words, and a pocket gets suggested when your words match what's already in it. it's instant and private, and it can guess wrong, so you always get to say yes or no thanks.

is it really free?

yes. it's open source under the MIT licence.

what if Ctrl + Shift + Space is already taken?

pick any shortcut you like in settings. tuck tells you if another app already has it.

does it work offline?

completely. it only needs the internet to check for updates.

is the buddy distracting?

it's small and calm. if you'd still rather not, tell it to stay put, or hide it from the tray. the popup works the same.

mac or linux?

not yet. windows 10 and 11 for now.

how do i move my thoughts to a new computer?

install tuck there, quit it, then copy your thoughts.json into the same folder.

get tuck

  1. open the latest release and download the file ending in -setup.exe
  2. open it. tuck is new, so windows might say it doesn't recognise it yet. click More info, then Run anyway
  3. click through the installer. no admin password needed
  4. press Ctrl + Shift + Space and tuck your first thought
